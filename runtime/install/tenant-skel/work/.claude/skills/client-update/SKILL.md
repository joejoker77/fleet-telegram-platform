# Update the client

## Establish what has changed since they last heard

```
~/work/bin/deal-brief <deal-id> --mail 12
```

Find the last email we sent the client, and work out what has happened on the
matter since. That, and only that, is the content of the update. An update that
restates what they already know is worse than no update.

If nothing has actually moved, say so to the fee earner rather than manufacturing
an email. "Nothing has changed since your 4 August email; do you want to tell
them that, or wait?" is the useful answer.

## Write it for the client, not for the file

- **No law without translation.** Not "we have submitted the ET1 within the
  primary limitation period" but "your claim went in to the tribunal on Tuesday,
  inside the deadline".
- **No Latin, no acronyms.** ACAS, ET1, ET3, WP, DoC and o/s all get spelled out
  the first time or replaced.
- **Answer the three questions every client is actually asking.** Where are we,
  how long will it take, and what do you need from me.
- **Give the next step a date.** "We expect their response by 12 September, and I
  will write to you when it comes" beats "we await their response in due course".
- **Say if you need nothing from them.** Clients read silence as a request.

Short paragraphs. No bullet points in a client email unless you are listing
documents they must provide.

## Costs and expectations

If the update touches money, be exact about which fee model this matter is on
and say nothing beyond what the retainer letter says. If you are unsure, leave it
out and tell the fee earner you left it out.

## Then stop

Show the draft to the fee earner. Do not send. This goes out under their name to
their client, and they sign it off, not you.
